Research analytics

Synthetic: every record in this domain is invented.

An invented research-analytics world, shaped like OpenAlex: works, the authors who wrote them, their institutions, the journals and repositories they appeared in, and the citations between them, as an analyst's extract from a bibliographic feed and an institution's own system would come. Everything is invented: the works, people, institutions, journals, ORCIDs, RORs, ISSNs and DOIs. Nothing comes from OpenAlex, so no answer can come from a model's memory. The codes (SUP, AFF, K0) are left cryptic, and nothing in the files says what they stand for.

The 48 questions are of two kinds:

  • Implicit meanings (Q1 to Q32, and Q47): what any research analyst knows without saying. One author can have several records, and two people can share a name. A lab belongs to its centre, its university and its university system. Full and fractional counting can rank institutions differently. An editorial or an erratum isn't a research article. A preprint and its published version are one work. Citations count within a time window, and a retracted work's don't count. Self-citation means something different for an author, an institution and a country.
  • Local meanings (Q33 to Q46, and Q48): rules this dataset sets for itself, stated only in the ontology. STG records are test data. SUP is a supplement article, not supplementary material. When an author lists several affiliations, only the one listed last is credited. The journal tiers count upwards, and K0 is a delisting. An affiliated institute (AFF) is not a unit of its parent. No model can know these without being told.

One ontology and one question bank are asked of both datasets. Each computes its own answer keys from its own data.

The datasets

The base data

85 works, 38 author records, 18 institutions, 8 sources, 138 authorships and 71 citations. 52 of the works are designed by hand so that each question has its point, and the other 33 are background. Small enough to read in full.

At 850 works

The base works plus 765 invented ones, 515 of which repeat the same traps many times over: more author records for the same people, more labs and affiliated institutes, more works with several affiliations, preprints, test records, retractions and delisted sources, and about 1,000 citations. That makes 850 works, 167 author records, 31 institutions, 14 sources, 1,532 authorships and 1,067 citations. The same 48 questions now need counting and joining across many records. scale.py builds it from a fixed seed.

Research domain: notes

The second domain for Phase 4: research analytics shaped like OpenAlex (works, authors, institutions, sources, authorships, citations). Same interface and files as domains/credit/, plus generate_data.py, which writes the CSV files.

Everything is invented: the works, people, institutions, journals, ORCIDs, RORs, ISSNs and DOIs. Nothing was downloaded from OpenAlex. The ORCIDs aren't checksummed, and the DOIs use the prefix 10.55555 (I haven't checked that the prefix is unassigned).

The local rules, and where the ontology states them

Each is stated only in the ontology, and none can be guessed from a code's name or from general conventions.

Rule Where it's stated Questions

Things I wasn't sure about

  • "Research work" is the dataset's own term. Most count questions ask for research works. Without the ontology, the model has to decide for itself what counts: that's the document-type trap. But it also means some R1 csv answers that count editorials could be called a reasonable reading rather than an error. Credit's "issuer rating" was the same.
  • Author self-citation is defined broadly: the citing and cited works share any author. So Q29's author count is 2 (Mara Ellison, and her co-author Priya Ramanathan, on W13). A model that counts only Mara's own citations says 1, which is a defensible reading without the ontology. Q29's institution count is also 2, by coincidence.
  • Citation windows have only two questions, and each question states the window ("its publication year and the two years after"), so the trap is only raw counts against windowed ones. No question sets a house window length. That could be a sixth local rule.
  • Citations from any type of work count (an editorial citing a paper is a citation), apart from test records and retracted works. No question depends on this choice.
  • Versions: a merged pair takes the published version's year, source and byline. The build checks that each preprint's credited authors match its published version's. canonical() merges author records by ORCID only. It doesn't merge a preprint with its published version, because Q20 asks which records are preprints, and merging would accept the wrong record of the pair.
  • Field-normalised citations, the "normalisation" trap planned for this domain, aren't covered: the data has no fields, and the trap needs a decimal answer.
  • The names are invented, but could collide with real ones. Some person names (Pieter Maas, Jonas Brandt) are common enough to belong to real people, and some place-based names (Albury, Dunmore) use real towns. Before publishing, check that no journal or institution name belongs to a real one.
  • Size: the RDF is about 75 KB, against 19 KB for credit, because authorships and citations are one resource per row. So R2 rdf and R6 rdf+ontology are the largest prompts (82 KB and 100 KB, about 20,000 to 25,000 tokens). To shrink them, citations could become a direct res:cites triple, but the conversion would then no longer be strictly one-resource-per-row.

Shared-file changes needed (not made, as asked)

  1. run.py: conditions per domain. CONDITIONS includes R10 executed and R7 csv+neutral, which this domain doesn't have, so a run without --only stops at once, with a ValueError naming the conditions it does have. Suggestion: domain.py here defines CONDITIONS, and run.py could default to getattr(domain, "CONDITIONS", CONDITIONS). The same applies to --only's choices.
  2. ontology_to_md.py: column names. It reads conv.ENTITY_COLS, conv.INSTRUMENT_COLS and conv.ACTION_COLS, which are the credit domain's tables, and catches only ImportError (a converter without these names would raise AttributeError). Here, csv_to_rdf.py defines its six tables' dicts and groups them onto those three names, so that no column name occurs twice with different properties in one group: three tables have a type column. Suggestion: a converter exposes COLUMNS = [dict, ...], and ontology_to_md.py uses it when present.
  3. ontology_to_md.py drops skos:definition on a skos:ConceptScheme. I moved the one sentence that was there (the tier order) onto the res:tier property, so the Markdown and the Turtle say the same thing.
  4. grade.py: a decimal kind for fractional counts and field-normalised scores, such as number, normalised with float() and compared within a tolerance (abs(got - want) <= 0.01). Until then, every fractional answer here is a whole number.
  5. README.md lists only domains/credit/ in its layout table.
  6. Phase 2 (query.py) loads CSV_FILES into DuckDB as tables named after the files (works, authorships, ...), so it should work here as it is. I haven't run it.
