Why is Valdoria Telecom's foreign-currency rating lower than its local-currency rating?

Tests: Ceilings: a Country Ceiling is a cap on foreign-currency ratings, not a rating of the sovereign or of the company.

Expected: Because of the Country Ceiling for Valdoria (BB), which caps the foreign-currency ratings of entities domiciled there (transfer and convertibility risk). The local-currency rating (BBB-) is not capped.

✅ R1 csv run 1 graded by llm

Valdoria's Country Ceiling is BB (A12), which caps the foreign-currency rating. Valdoria Telecom's FC rating is therefore BB (A14), while its LC rating is BBB- (A13).

✅ R1 csv run 2 graded by llm

The country ceiling for Valdoria is BB (A12), which caps foreign-currency ratings (E05 FC BB, A14). The local-currency rating is BBB- (A13).

🟡 R1 csv run 3 graded by llm

The data doesn't state a reason. E05's FC rating is BB (A14), the same as Valdoria's Country Ceiling BB (A12), while LC is BBB- (A13). That is consistent with the ceiling capping FC only.

Why: Identifies the BB Country Ceiling matching the FC rating and that it is consistent with capping FC only, but hedges by saying the data doesn't state the reason rather than naming the ceiling as the cause.

🟡 R1 csv run 4 graded by llm

The data indicates a country-ceiling effect. E05's foreign-currency rating is BB, equal to Valdoria's Country Ceiling of BB (A12), while its local-currency rating is BBB- (A13, A14). The data gives no explicit reason.

Why: Names the BB Country Ceiling as matching the FC rating, but does not state that the ceiling caps foreign-currency and not local-currency ratings, and hedges that no explicit reason is given.
